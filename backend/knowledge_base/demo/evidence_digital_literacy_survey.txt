National Digital Literacy Survey (Synthetic) — Summary Report

Methodology (fictional): A synthetic household survey of 4,000 respondents across
12 fictional districts, designed for demonstration purposes only.

Key findings:
- 41% of respondents in low-income districts report no reliable home internet
  connection.
- 58% of respondents aged 55+ report low confidence completing an online form
  without assistance.
- 76% of respondents who had used a physical government service centre in the
  past year said they would prefer a hybrid (in-person plus digital) option over
  a fully digital one.

Connectivity constraints

Rural and low-income districts show materially lower broadband penetration than
urban districts in this synthetic sample. Mobile data coverage is more consistent
than fixed broadband, but data cost is cited as a barrier by 33% of respondents.

Digital literacy gaps

Respondents without prior exposure to online government services required an
average of three assisted sessions before completing a task independently in
this synthetic pilot. This suggests that a single onboarding session may be
insufficient for a meaningful share of the target population.

Implications for programme design (analyst commentary, not raw survey data)

Programmes assuming immediate self-service adoption without an assisted-channel
fallback risk excluding the digitally underserved segment identified above. A
phased approach with in-person support during an initial adoption period is
associated with higher completion rates in comparable synthetic scenarios
modeled for this report.
