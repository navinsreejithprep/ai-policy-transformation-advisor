International Benchmark (Synthetic): Estlandia Digital Access Initiative

Objective: Increase digital public-service completion rates among underserved
citizens, launched in a fictional programme year for illustrative purposes.

Approach: Estlandia combined a national digital ID with a network of
community-based "digital access points" staffed by trained facilitators,
rather than relying on self-service adoption alone.

Implementation model: Delivery was run jointly by the fictional Ministry of
Digital Affairs and municipal governments, with facilitators contracted through
local NGOs rather than a single national vendor.

[PAGE 2]
Funding and governance (synthetic)

Funded through a three-year ring-fenced digital transformation budget line, with
disbursement tied to milestone completion rather than a single upfront grant. A
joint steering committee including citizen representatives reviewed adoption
metrics quarterly.

Outcomes (synthetic, illustrative only)

In this fictional case, self-reported task-completion confidence rose over the
programme period among surveyed users of the access points, and reliance on
in-person-only completion declined as digital confidence grew. These figures are
illustrative and were constructed for this demo, not measured.

[PAGE 3]
Lessons (analyst commentary)

- Facilitator-supported access points appear more effective than self-service
  portals alone when digital literacy is uneven across the population.
- Milestone-based funding tied to adoption metrics, rather than flat grants,
  encouraged continuous monitoring in this fictional case.
- Comparability caveat: Estlandia's programme assumed an existing national
  digital ID system; a programme without that foundation would need to budget
  for identity verification separately before this comparison can be treated as
  directly transferable.
